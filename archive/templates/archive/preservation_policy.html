Preservation Policy

Why this archive exists, and how we handle what we collect

Why This Exists

Some people collect CDs. Some collect video games. Some collect a very specific kind of vintage toy. Those hobbies usually look sane to outsiders because the objects are already framed as valuable.

This is ours. Collecting and preserving furry convention materials—especially conbooks—is a special interest. The archive is the public product of that. It is not always easy to explain. To a lot of people it looks obsessive, or weird, or like caring too much about paper that was meant to be disposable. We cannot simply swap it for a more socially acceptable hobby. What we can do is turn that focus into something useful: digitize the material, keep it findable, and stop it from vanishing quietly.

These items are printed in low quantities. They get thrown out by attendees who are done with them, discarded by conventions that no longer know what to do with leftover stock, or forgotten in a drawer for twenty years until someone moves and the box goes with the trash. None of that is usually cruelty. Most of the time it is simply that nobody has a plan for the clutter—and by the time someone does, the window has already closed.

Our Mission

The Furry Con Archives preserves and makes accessible historical documents from the furry fandom. We believe these materials are culturally significant and worth keeping available for people who care about them—now and later.

We try to balance preservation with respect for creators, copyright holders, and the community. We do not claim ownership of the materials we host.

What We Archive

We collect and preserve:

  • Convention booklets, programs, and related materials
  • Newsletters and community publications
  • Historical documents and ephemera
  • Publicly distributed materials from furry conventions and events

We prioritize materials that are publicly distributed, historically meaningful to the fandom, and at real risk of being lost—whether through disposal, neglect, or simple forgetfulness.

Why These Materials Disappear

Smaller and older conventions often printed only a few hundred copies of a conbook. Survival rates matter. Unlike mass-market books, losing a handful of originals can erase an entire year of that event's paper record.

  • Thrown out: Attendees clear shelves. Cons clear leftover inventory. From the outside it looks like clutter; from the inside of this hobby, it is often the last copy anyone will ever see.
  • Forgotten: Boxes sit in closets, storage units, or volunteer houses for years. Nobody is actively destroying them—they just stop being anyone's problem until a move, a flood, or a leadership change decides for them.
  • Physical wear: Cheap paper yellows and goes brittle. Staples rust and stain pages. Uncontrolled storage (basements, attics, garages) speeds all of that up. Once the paper is too fragile to handle, digitization gets much harder.
  • Lost digital originals: Many 2000s-era books were laid out digitally, then the source files vanished with old drives, dead formats, or organizers who moved on. The printed copy someone took home is frequently the only trustworthy record left of how the book actually looked.

We are not claiming conventions are villains. Most are event organizers, not archives. Hosting a weekend is already a huge job. Keeping decades of leftovers forever—without a clear home for them—is something almost nobody is staffed or funded to do. When leftover stock becomes "clutter with no next step," disposal is the default. We exist so there is another option.

Donating Materials

If you have conbooks, zines, flyers, leftover convention stock, or a box you inherited and do not know what to do with: we want to hear from you. One copy matters. A whole shelf matters. Condition does not have to be perfect.

  • Individuals can donate personal collections or single items
  • Conventions can send leftover stock, older inventory, or materials they no longer have room or reason to keep
  • Loan-to-digitize is possible when you want scans but prefer to keep originals

Informal drop-off bins at events rarely work well on their own. Things arrive unlabeled, mixed with random handouts, and then sit until someone figures out the next step—which often never comes. Direct contact is better: we can say what we need, what we already have, and how to get materials here without them vanishing into another unmarked box.

Contact us and we will work out logistics. Shipping support may be available depending on the situation.

Copyright and Fair Use

We respect intellectual property rights and operate under principles of fair use for educational and preservation purposes. Our archive:

  • Makes materials available for research and educational purposes
  • Does not claim ownership of archived materials
  • Provides attribution when possible
  • Respects takedown requests from rights holders

If you believe your copyrighted material has been included in the archive without proper authorization, please contact us to request removal.

Takedown Requests

We take copyright and content concerns seriously. If you are a rights holder and wish to have content removed from the archive, we will process your request promptly.

To submit a takedown request, please contact us:

dmca@furryconarchives.org
DMCA Email

When submitting a takedown request, please include:

  • Your name and contact information
  • Identification of the specific document(s) to be removed
  • Your relationship to the content (author, copyright holder, etc.)
  • Reason for the takedown request

We will review and respond to all legitimate takedown requests within a reasonable timeframe.

Privacy and Data

We are committed to protecting privacy:

  • We do not collect personal information beyond what is necessary for archive operations
  • PDF view statistics are anonymized
  • We respect the privacy of document donors and contributors
  • Personal information in archived documents is preserved as-is, but we do not actively collect or index it

Community Guidelines

The Furry Con Archives is a community resource. We ask users to:

  • Respect the materials and their creators
  • Use archived materials responsibly
  • Report any concerns or issues
  • Help preserve furry fandom history by contributing when possible
